Araceae

The arum family

Recognised by a flower structure unlike most other families: a spike of tiny flowers (the spadix) wrapped in a single petal-like bract (the spathe). Leaves are often glossy and, in climbing genera, develop distinctive perforations with age.

Distribution: Widespread, with greatest diversity in tropical regions.

Rosaceae

The rose family

A large family typically bearing five-petalled flowers with a whorl of stamens, ranging from thorned shrubs to fruit-bearing trees. Many of the plants most associated with orchards and hedgerows sit here.

Distribution: Worldwide, especially in temperate regions.

Fabaceae

The legume family

Identified by pod fruits and, in many genera, compound leaves that fold at night. Roots in this family commonly host bacteria that fix nitrogen into the surrounding soil.

Distribution: Nearly worldwide, from tropical forests to temperate regions.

Asparagaceae

A family redrawn more than once

A broad family of strap- or blade-shaped leaves with parallel veins, brought together from several older, smaller families as classification has been revised. Boundaries here shift more than most.

Distribution: Worldwide, with many species in tropical and subtropical regions.
