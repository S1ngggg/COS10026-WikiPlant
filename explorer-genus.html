Genera in the index

A genus groups plants close enough to share a first name in their scientific classification. Each one below is filed under its family, with the species logged inside it.

Verify your plant

Family Genus Species

Araceae

← back to family record

Monstera

Climbing aroids that root as they grow, best known for the wide leaf perforations that develop with maturity.

📍 Native range example: Panama
Family: Araceae
M. deliciosa M. acuminata

Spathiphyllum

Compact aroids grown for a single curved white spathe wrapped around a pale spadix.

📍 Native range example: Colombia
Family: Araceae
S. wallisii S. cannifolium

Rosaceae

← back to family record

Rosa

Shrubs and climbers with five-petalled flowers; many species bear prickles, though some, such as Lady Banks' rose, do not.

📍 Example native range (Rosa banksiae): China
Family: Rosaceae
R. canina R. banksiae

Fragaria

Low, spreading plants with trifoliate leaves, grown for the small fruit that follows their white flowers.

📍 Native range example: Europe
Family: Rosaceae
F. vesca F. moschata

Fabaceae

← back to family record

Lathyrus

Herbaceous legumes with paired leaflets and tendrils; species include the garden pea and many ornamental sweet peas.

📍 Example native range (garden pea): Europe
Family: Fabaceae
L. oleraceus L. fulvus

Trifolium

Herbaceous legumes with three-part leaves and rounded flower heads; many species are grown as forage and cover crops.

📍 Native range example: Europe
Family: Fabaceae
T. pratense T. repens

Asparagaceae

← back to family record

Dracaena

Rosette or upright plants with stiff, strap-like leaves, several formerly classed under Sansevieria.

📍 Native range example: Socotra Archipelago
Family: Asparagaceae
D. trifasciata D. cinnabari

Chlorophytum

Arching, grass-like leaves that send out long runners tipped with plantlets.

📍 Native range example: South Africa
Family: Asparagaceae
C. comosum C. tuberosum
