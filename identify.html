Plant Details

Common Name
Dog Rose
Scientific Name
Rosa canina L.
Family
Rosaceae
Genus
Rosa
Species
Rosa canina
Plant Type
Deciduous, perennial flowering shrub with arching, prickly stems.

Morphological Characteristics

Growth Habit
A vigorous, arching or scrambling shrub that may climb over surrounding vegetation for support.
Height
Typically 1–3 m, occasionally taller when supported by other plants.
Leaves
Pinnately compound leaves, usually consisting of 5–7 oval to elliptical green leaflets.
Leaf Arrangement
Alternate, with leaves arranged individually along the stems.
Leaf Margin
Serrated (toothed), with small, pointed teeth along the edges of each leaflet.
Stem
Long, arching woody stems bearing sharp, curved or hooked prickles.
Flowers
Usually pale pink or white, with five petals and numerous yellow stamens surrounding the central reproductive structures.
Fruit
Oval, red to orange-red rose hips, typically 15–20 mm long, containing numerous small fruits (achenes).
Aroma
Flowers generally have a faint, sweet fragrance.

Habitat & Distribution

Dog Rose is native to much of Europe, northwestern Africa, and parts of western and central Asia. It commonly grows in hedgerows, woodland edges, scrubland, and open grasslands.

Care Tips

  • Watering: Water during prolonged dry periods, especially while the plant is becoming established. Avoid waterlogged soil.
  • Sunlight: Grow in full sun or partial shade, with sunnier positions generally encouraging better flowering.
  • Soil: Use fertile, moist but well-drained soil.
  • Temperature: Dog Rose is a hardy temperate shrub that tolerates winter frost and seasonal cold conditions.

Identification Notes

Dog Rose can resemble other wild rose species, making identification based on flower appearance alone unreliable. Examine the combination of leaflets, hooked prickles, sepals, and rose hips, and compare these characteristics with reliable botanical references before confirming the species.
